Methodology

Data sources, cleaning decisions, database design, and known limitations.

Data sources

All quantitative data comes from Eurostat, the statistical office of the European Union. Numerical datasets were downloaded in SDMX-CSV 2.0 format (gzipped) from the Eurostat bulk download facility, with the urban-rural classification taken from the official NUTS 2021 reference spreadsheet. All raw files are stored unmodified in data/raw/.

Scope and filtering

  • Geographic unit: NUTS-2 regions (4-character Eurostat codes). NUTS-0 (countries), NUTS-1, and NUTS-3 sub-regions are excluded from the outcome tables. The 27 EU member states are included; candidate and non-member countries are excluded. Greece uses the prefix "EL" per Eurostat convention.
  • Temporal scope: For each dataset, the most recent year with at least 80% of EU-27 NUTS-2 regions reporting a valid value is selected. If no year meets that threshold, the year with the most observations is used.
  • Value selection: Only rows with non-null OBS_VALUE are kept. Missing values in Eurostat datasets may indicate that a region is not classifiable under that typology, or that data collection was incomplete.
  • Annual frequency: Only freq = "A" (annual) observations are used; quarterly or monthly breakdowns are not considered.

Dataset-specific cleaning notes

Transport infrastructure — tran_r_net

Filtered to tra_infr = "RL" (total railway line length) and "MWAY" (motorways). Unit KM_TKM2 (kilometres per thousand square kilometres) is used directly — this is already a density measure, removing the need to join area data. Year used: 2024.

Vehicle stock — tran_r_vehst

Filtered to vehicle = "CAR" (passenger cars) and unit = "P_THAB" (per thousand inhabitants). This gives the motorisation rate directly, without needing to divide by population. Year used: 2024.

Employment rate — lfst_r_lfe2emprt

Working-age employment rate: age = "Y20-64", sex = "T" (total), unit = "PC" (percentage). Year used: 2024.

Unemployment rate — lfst_r_lfu3rt

Broad unemployment rate: age = "Y15-74", sex = "T", isced11 = "TOTAL" (all education levels), unit = "PC". Year used: 2024.

Tertiary attainment — edat_lfse_04

Share of population aged 25–34 with tertiary education (ISCED 5–8): isced11 = "ED5-8", age = "Y25-34", sex = "T". Year used: 2024.

Early school leavers — edat_lfse_16

Share of 18–24 year-olds who have left education without an upper secondary qualification: age = "Y18-24", sex = "T". Year used: 2019 (best coverage available).

Life expectancy — demo_r_mlifexp

Life expectancy at birth: age = "Y_LT1" (less than 1 year old = at birth), sex = "T", unit = "YR" (years). Year used: 2024.

Physicians — hlth_rs_prsrg

Medical doctors per 100,000 inhabitants: isco08 = "OC221" (medical doctors), unit = "P_HTHAB". This dataset is historical and ends in 2020/2021; year used: 2016 (best NUTS-2 EU-27 coverage).

GDP per capita — nama_10r_2gdp

GDP per inhabitant in purchasing power standards: unit = "PPS_EU27_2020_HAB" (PPS per inhabitant, EU27 2020 baseline). PPS adjusts for cross-country price-level differences so a Bulgarian and a Dutch figure are directly comparable. Year used: 2024.

Workplace, not residence. Eurostat records GDP where economic output is generated, not where workers live. Capital regions like Île-de-France, Inner London (pre-Brexit), and Luxembourg therefore appear far wealthier than their resident populations are, because commuters from neighbouring regions contribute to the headline number. This is a feature of the data, not a bug, and it actually reinforces the connectivity argument: rural workers can only access high-productivity urban jobs if they can physically reach them.

Urban-rural typology

Urban-rural classification comes from the official Eurostat NUTS 2021 urban-rural typology, downloaded from the Eurostat NUTS reference spreadsheet (NUTS2021.xlsx, sheet Urban-rural). Each NUTS-3 region is assigned one of three categories:

  • Predominantly urban: at least 80% of the population lives in urban grid cells.
  • Intermediate: between 50% and 80% urban population.
  • Predominantly rural: more than half of the population lives in rural grid cells.

All outcome data in this project (employment, education, health, transport) is reported at NUTS-2 level, while the typology lives at NUTS-3. To bring them onto the same axis, each NUTS-2 region is assigned a dominant typology equal to the most common category among its NUTS-3 children. Ties are broken in favour of the more-urban category — a NUTS-2 region with equal urban and rural NUTS-3 children is labelled urban on the grounds that the anchor city dominates regional outcomes.

The dominant typology is computed once at load time and stored as a denormalised urban_rural_typology column on nuts2_regions, alongside the full per-NUTS-3 table. This preserves the proper one-to-many relational structure (1 NUTS-2 → many NUTS-3, 242 NUTS-2 parents covering 1,166 NUTS-3 regions in EU-27) while keeping dashboard queries simple.

Boundary version mismatch. The typology spreadsheet uses NUTS 2021 boundaries; some Eurostat datasets we consume already use NUTS 2024. Seven NUTS-2 codes (notably new Dutch and Portuguese subdivisions) appear in the 2024 data but have no published NUTS 2021 typology. These regions, plus extra-regio “ZZ” aggregate codes, are dropped during the load step (65 codes total). Eurostat has not yet published a machine-readable NUTS 2024 typology.

Database design

Data is stored in a SQLite relational database (database/the_15_minute_divide.db) with 7 tables and foreign-key constraints. Raw SQLite3 is used throughout — no ORM. 

countries (1) ──< (many) nuts2_regions
nuts2_regions (1) ──< (many) nuts3_regions
nuts2_regions (1) ──< (many) transport_infrastructure
nuts2_regions (1) ──< (many) employment_outcomes
nuts2_regions (1) ──< (many) education_outcomes
nuts2_regions (1) ──< (many) health_outcomes

All Flask routes use parameterised queries (? placeholders) to prevent SQL injection. The sqlite3.Row factory is used so query results behave as dictionaries when passed to Jinja templates or serialised as JSON.

Limitations

  • Causation vs. correlation. All relationships shown are correlational. Transport infrastructure may be a consequence of urbanisation rather than a cause of better outcomes.
  • Cross-country comparability. Raw indicators are not z-score normalised across countries. A Dutch baseline and a Bulgarian baseline are structurally different. Country-level charts are provided for within-country comparison.
  • Temporal mismatch. Indicators come from different years (2016–2024). Causal inferences across time are not intended.
  • Proxy typology. The urban-rural classification is employment-derived, not the official Eurostat NUTS-3 typology. Capital-city regions are hardcoded as urban; remaining regions are classified by tertile.
  • Missing data. Not all NUTS-2 regions have data for all indicators. Charts display only regions with available data and do not impute missing values.
