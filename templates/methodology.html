Methodology

Data sources, cleaning decisions, database design, and known limitations.

Data sources

All quantitative indicators come from Eurostat, the statistical office of the European Union. The project uses nine gzipped SDMX-CSV 2.0 datasets for transport, labour, education, health, and economic indicators, plus the official NUTS 2021 urban-rural typology spreadsheet. Raw files are stored unmodified in data/raw/; the loader applies filters, selects the best coverage year, and writes the cleaned values to SQLite.

Scope and filtering

  • Geographic unit: the analysis table contains 242 retained EU-27 NUTS-2 regions, identified by 4-character Eurostat codes. NUTS-0 countries, NUTS-1 macro-regions, NUTS-3 sub-regions, candidate countries, and non-member countries are excluded from the outcome tables. Greece uses the Eurostat country prefix "EL", not "GR".
  • Temporal scope: For each dataset, the most recent year with at least 80% valid coverage among reporting EU-27 NUTS-2 regions is selected. If no year meets that threshold, the year with the most valid observations is used. This gives 2024 for most indicators, 2019 for early school leavers, and 2016 for physicians. Selected years are tracked per metric because some database tables group two indicators from different source years.
  • Value selection: Only rows with non-null OBS_VALUE are kept. Missing values in Eurostat datasets are not imputed, converted to zero, or carried forward.
  • Aggregation: averages shown in the charts are unweighted regional means. They describe the average NUTS-2 region, not the average EU resident.

Dataset-specific cleaning notes

Transport infrastructure — tran_r_net

Filtered to tra_infr = "RL" (total railway line length) and "MWAY" (motorways). Unit KM_TKM2 (kilometres per thousand square kilometres) is used directly — this is already a density measure, removing the need to join area data. Year used: 2024.

Vehicle stock — tran_r_vehst

Filtered to vehicle = "CAR" (passenger cars) and unit = "P_THAB" (per thousand inhabitants). This gives the motorisation rate directly, without needing to divide by population. Year used: 2024.

Employment rate — lfst_r_lfe2emprt

Working-age employment rate: age = "Y20-64", sex = "T" (total), unit = "PC" (percentage). Year used: 2024.

Unemployment rate — lfst_r_lfu3rt

Broad unemployment rate: age = "Y15-74", sex = "T", isced11 = "TOTAL" (all education levels), unit = "PC". Year used: 2024.

Tertiary attainment — edat_lfse_04

Share of population aged 25–34 with tertiary education (ISCED 5–8): isced11 = "ED5-8", age = "Y25-34", sex = "T". Year used: 2024.

Early school leavers — edat_lfse_16

Share of 18–24 year-olds who have left education without an upper secondary qualification: age = "Y18-24", sex = "T". Year used: 2019 (best coverage available).

Life expectancy — demo_r_mlifexp

Life expectancy at birth: age = "Y_LT1" (less than 1 year old = at birth), sex = "T", unit = "YR" (years). Year used: 2024.

Physicians — hlth_rs_prsrg

Medical doctors per 100,000 inhabitants: isco08 = "OC221" (medical doctors), unit = "P_HTHAB". This dataset is historical and ends in 2020/2021; year used: 2016 (best NUTS-2 EU-27 coverage).

GDP per capita — nama_10r_2gdp

GDP per inhabitant in purchasing power standards: unit = "PPS_EU27_2020_HAB" (PPS per inhabitant, EU27 2020 baseline). PPS adjusts for cross-country price-level differences so a Bulgarian and a Dutch figure are directly comparable. Year used: 2024.

Workplace, not residence. Regional GDP is assigned where economic output is generated. Capital and major employment regions can therefore look wealthier than the income of their resident population alone would suggest, because commuters from neighbouring regions contribute to the output measured there.

Urban-rural typology

Urban-rural classification comes from the official Eurostat NUTS 2021 urban-rural typology, downloaded from the Eurostat NUTS reference spreadsheet (NUTS2021.xlsx, sheet Urban-rural). Each NUTS-3 region is assigned one of three categories:

  • Predominantly urban: at least 80% of the population lives in urban clusters.
  • Intermediate: more than 50% but less than 80% of the population lives in urban clusters.
  • Predominantly rural: at least 50% of the population lives in rural grid cells.

The indicators used in this project are reported at NUTS-2 level, while the typology is supplied at NUTS-3 level. To bring them onto the same axis, each NUTS-2 region is assigned a dominant typology equal to the most common category among its NUTS-3 children. This is a count-based majority roll-up, not a population-weighted roll-up. Ties are broken in favour of the more-urban category.

The dominant typology is computed once at load time and stored as a denormalised urban_rural_typology column on nuts2_regions, alongside the full per-NUTS-3 table. This preserves the one-to-many relational structure (1 NUTS-2 → many NUTS-3, 242 retained NUTS-2 parents covering 1,166 EU-27 NUTS-3 regions) while keeping dashboard queries simple.

Boundary version mismatch. The current project data uses the NUTS 2021 typology file, while several Eurostat indicator files already contain NUTS 2024 regional codes. The loader treats the NUTS 2021 typology as the regional universe: any four-character code not present there is skipped before insertion. In the current load, 65 such codes are dropped and 242 NUTS-2 regions remain. Updating the raw typology source to NUTS 2024 would be the next maintenance step.

Database design

Data is stored in a SQLite relational database (database/the_15_minute_divide.db) with 8 core analytical tables and foreign-key constraints. Raw SQLite3 is used throughout — no ORM. The runtime also creates three tracking tables for page-view analytics; those are separate from the Eurostat analytical schema. 

countries (1) ──< (many) nuts2_regions
nuts2_regions (1) ──< (many) nuts3_regions
nuts2_regions (1) ──< (many) transport_infrastructure
nuts2_regions (1) ──< (many) employment_outcomes
nuts2_regions (1) ──< (many) education_outcomes
nuts2_regions (1) ──< (many) health_outcomes
nuts2_regions (1) ──< (many) economic_outcomes

All Flask routes use parameterised queries (? placeholders) to prevent SQL injection. The sqlite3.Row factory is used so query results behave as dictionaries when passed to Jinja templates or serialised as JSON.

Limitations

  • Causation vs. correlation. All relationships shown are correlational. Transport infrastructure may be a consequence of urbanisation rather than a cause of better outcomes.
  • Cross-country comparability. Indicators are not z-score normalised across countries. GDP is purchasing-power adjusted, but institutional systems, settlement patterns, and national baselines still differ.
  • Temporal mismatch. Indicators come from different years (2016–2024). Causal inferences across time are not intended.
  • Typology roll-up. Eurostat supplies the urban-rural typology at NUTS-3 level. The NUTS-2 label used in charts is a derived dominant category, so mixed regions are simplified into one label.
  • Railway density proxy. Railway density measures kilometres of railway line per 1,000 km². It does not measure service frequency, journey time, affordability, reliability, or whether stations are usable for the people living nearby.
  • Missing data. Not all NUTS-2 regions have data for all indicators. Charts display only regions with available data and do not impute missing values.
