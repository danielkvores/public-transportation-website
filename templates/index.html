The Air We Breathe

The map below shows the concentration of fine particulate matter (PM2.5) — the most harmful common air pollutant — across European regions. Darker areas indicate higher pollution levels and a greater risk of premature death.

Notice the pattern: southern and eastern Europe — particularly the Po Valley in Italy, industrial Poland, Bulgaria, and the Balkans — carry the heaviest burden.

The Divide

Air pollution in Europe is not just an environmental issue — it is a question of inequality. The poorest regions of the EU are exposed to significantly higher levels of PM2.5 than the wealthiest, and they suffer more deaths as a result.

[ Comparative visualisations will be rendered here in Stage II ]

The causes are structural: reliance on solid fuels for heating, older and more polluting vehicle fleets, industrial concentration, and lower investment in environmental regulation. The people producing Europe's goods and heating their homes with coal are breathing the worst air on the continent.
