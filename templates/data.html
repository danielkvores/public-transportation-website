Data Explorer

Explore how transport access, income, education, work, and health vary across Europe’s regions. Choose a country, compare urban and rural areas, and see where national averages hide the sharpest regional divides.

01

A Country, Region by Region

Select a country to see how its regions compare with the national average and the EU average. The spread often matters as much as the headline number: some countries look balanced, while others contain regions moving at very different speeds.

Select a country to load its regional data

Dots show NUTS-2 regions. The red reference line is the selected country average; the dark dashed line is the EU-27 average. Dot colours keep the urban/intermediate/rural typology. Missing metrics are hidden instead of being treated as zero.

02

Urban and Rural Europe, Split by Income

This chart separates countries into lower- and higher-income groups, then compares urban, intermediate, and rural regions within each group. It shows whether the urban-rural divide looks different in richer and poorer parts of Europe.

Source: Eurostat nama_10r_2gdp (GDP per capita, PPS), tran_r_net (railway density), lfst_r_lfe2emprt (employment), demo_r_mlifexp (life expectancy), edat_lfse_04 / edat_lfse_16 (education). Country split is based on average regional GDP per capita.

03

Does More Rail Come With Better Outcomes?

Regions are grouped by railway density, from the lowest to the highest rail coverage. Use the tabs to compare whether denser rail networks tend to align with stronger economic, education, employment, or health outcomes.

Source: Eurostat tran_r_net (2024) and selected outcome datasets. Only regions with railway-density data are included in the bands.

04

Where National Averages Hide the Biggest Gaps

Countries are ranked by the distance between their highest- and lowest-performing regions for each indicator. Longer ranges point to countries where regional inequality is especially visible inside the same national border.

Each horizontal range runs from the lowest to highest NUTS-2 region in a country. The pale dot marks the lowest region; the red dot marks the highest. Countries with fewer than two available regional values for the selected metric are excluded from that view.
