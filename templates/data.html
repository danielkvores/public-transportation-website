All charts pull live from the database. Each point is a NUTS-2 region. Hover for region names. Use the country selector to zoom in.

01

Urban, Intermediate, Rural — The Core Divide

Average outcomes for EU NUTS-2 regions grouped by their official Eurostat urban-rural typology. GDP per capita is where the gap is widest: urban regions produce far more economic output per person than rural ones, even though employment rates are nearly identical. Life expectancy and education tell the same story in years and percentage points.

Source: Eurostat nama_10r_2gdp (GDP per capita, PPS), lfst_r_lfe2emprt (employment), demo_r_mlifexp (life expectancy), edat_lfse_04 / edat_lfse_16 (education). Typology from the official Eurostat NUTS 2021 urban-rural classification (see Methodology). GDP per capita is reported at workplace, not residence — capital regions are inflated by commuters.

02

Connected Regions Work More

Scatter plot of railway density (km of track per 1,000 km²) against employment rate for each NUTS-2 region. Colour indicates urban-rural classification. The upward slope is the structural argument: connectivity and employment move together.

Source: Eurostat tran_r_net (2024), lfst_r_lfe2emprt (2024). Hover over a point to see the region name.

04

The Top and Bottom 30 Regions

The 30 highest- and 30 lowest-employment NUTS-2 regions in the EU. Notice how rural regions cluster at the bottom, urban at the top — with only rare exceptions.

Source: Eurostat lfst_r_lfe2emprt (2024). Colour: urban = blue, intermediate = teal, rural = amber.
