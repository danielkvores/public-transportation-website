About This Project

Purpose

This website investigates the unequal distribution of air pollution and its health consequences across Europe. Its central argument is that where you live — and how wealthy your region is — determines the air you breathe and the years it costs you.

Using publicly available data from European and international institutions, the site shows that Europe's poorest regions bear a disproportionate pollution burden, driven by solid fuel heating, ageing vehicle fleets, and industrial concentration. While the assingment does suggest using multiple pages, I've opted to keep the majority of the information one a single page. I think it's easier to tell a "story" to the user; they are progressing by scrolling and they don't get taken out of the flow

Data Sources

The project will probably draw on three primary datasets, linked through a relational database on country and regional (NUTS3) identifiers and year:

  • EEA Air Quality Health Risk Assessments — PM2.5 concentrations, premature deaths, and years of life lost at country and NUTS3 level across 41 European countries (2005–2023). Source: European Environment Agency.
  • Eurostat Regional GDP — GDP per capita at purchasing power standard at NUTS3 level, used to classify regions by wealth quintile. Source: Eurostat.
  • World Bank Development Indicators — Industry value added (% of GDP), urbanisation rates, fossil fuel consumption, and population data at country level. Source: World Bank WDI.
